Outcome vs. Workflow Design study by Anna Kosareva, November 2026

Alex Chen has a problem. Two IT support systems handle it.

“I can’t open the page.”

Both follow the same security rules.
The difference is how they figure out what to do next.

AI automation

The form decides

AI-native

The state decides

The agent works out the case and asks for what is missing. A fixed workflow runs the rest and enforces the rules.

  • Human a person acts
  • Automation fixed code, rules, forms, templates
  • AI the model reads or chooses
  • AI + automation the agent chooses, automation runs and enforces it

Policy: who may have what, and who approves. Template: what a request must contain and what happens next, written by the support team from the policy and behind the form.

Expected paths, not measurements. These flows are design models. AI automation is modeled as a form and a fixed workflow, not as the Jira code in this repository. Recorded runs and audit-log counts will replace the expected numbers, including where they disagree. Every service shown is simulated.

Where each design is strong, and where it isn’t

Neither wins everywhere. Both wait for a person to approve; the rest comes down to who works out the case, who keeps the rules current, and how you find out what happened.

Topic AI automation AI-native
Who works out who can help?
AI automation Weak

Alex does. He has to realize he needs access and find the right form himself.

AI-native Strong

The agent does. It works out the case from what Alex says and what it can check.

Is it really an access problem?
AI automation Weak

Not checked. The form runs even if Alex already has access or the system is down.

AI-native Strong

Checked first: does he already have access, and is the system up?

Where does the required information live?
AI automation Trade-off

In the form’s template, which the support team writes from the policy and updates by hand when the policy changes. Every field is required, justification included, so nothing comes back for more.

AI-native Trade-off

In the policy’s system defaults, so Alex isn’t asked what the policy knows. The justification still comes from him, so the agent asks for it in chat. A policy change needs no template edit.

Keeping the rules current
AI automation Weak

The support team updates the forms and templates by hand when the policy changes. Triggering that update from the policy may not be possible.

AI-native Strong

The agent reads the policy directly. A change applies at once.

How Alex is told
AI automation Trade-off

Notifications from the form system, in fixed wording: waiting, done and declined. Predictable and on record, but it can’t answer a question he asks back.

AI-native Trade-off

Replies in the same chat where he asked. The agent writes the questions and explanations. Waiting, ready and declined are fixed text from the workflow, so the key messages can’t drift.

Tracking the issue
AI automation Strong

The request record follows every step, with the justification and approval attached. The workflow can also log its key steps.

AI-native Trade-off

The request is saved with a status, and the agent’s own logs feed metrics and dashboards. A view for non-engineers has to be built on top.

When it breaks
AI automation Strong

Anyone can open the request, see which step it stopped at and what to do next.

AI-native Weak

Someone has to read the logs. There is no request page to look at.

Also worth weighing
What does the model add?
AI automation Trade-off

Nothing, by design: the form is plain automation. It is cheap and predictable, and it can’t read “I can’t open the page” or decide which checks to run.

AI-native Strong

Reading the message, choosing the checks and asking only for what is missing. The steps after that are a fixed workflow, not chosen by the model.

Upkeep of the system itself
AI automation Trade-off

Forms, templates and a fixed workflow. Generating them from a structured policy would shrink the work.

AI-native Weak

A few read-only tools, prompts, tests and model updates, plus the workflow behind it. The policy has to be structured data, not prose.

Audit trail and hand-off
AI automation Strong

The request record is the audit trail and the place a person picks the case up, justification included. The workflow can log its key steps too.

AI-native Trade-off

Logs must be durable and readable by auditors. When the agent can’t resolve a case, a person needs somewhere to pick it up: a request record only for exceptions would do.

Cost and predictability
AI automation Strong

Predictable: no model calls at all (expected). The cost is upkeep, wasted approvals and Alex’s manual steps.

AI-native Weak

A model call for each reading and asking step, about 4 for a normal request (expected), each carrying the instructions and tools. Answers can vary between runs, and it needs testing and security work.

Adding more request types
AI automation Weak

Each new request type needs its own form and template.

AI-native Trade-off

A new type needs policy rules and a workflow, and every new type widens what the model can touch.

Same for both versions
Can it grant access without approval?
Same for both

No. The identity provider re-checks that an approval exists, was approved, came from the policy-resolved approver and matches user, system and level. AI-native never wins by skipping a control.

Waiting for approval
Same for both

A person still has to approve, so the manager’s answer sets the pace in either approach.

Side topic: fewer approvals
Policy decision

The company could put it in the policy: grant the standard access at onboarding, or approve automatically for specific departments. That removes the longest wait in both versions, and it is a policy choice, not an architecture one.

Model calls per scenario

Cost in a model-based system is roughly the number of calls times the tokens in each call. These counts come from the scenario steps, not from recorded runs. They are expected, not measured.

Per call: AI automation makes no model calls: the form and workflow are plain automation. The AI-native calls each carry the instructions, the tool list and the policy data, so each one is large. Expect AI-native to cost more in model tokens on every request. Where it saves is people’s time and wasted approvals. Real numbers will come from the logs of the recorded runs.

The model proposes. Code decides.

The comparison is only fair if both versions sit on the same ground. They share the controls and differ in how the work is chosen.

Shared by both versions

  • Policy. One approval matrix and one approver resolution.
  • Enforcement. The identity provider checks the approval before any grant.
  • Audit. An append-only log with a correlation ID per run.
  • Approval. The same email with Approve and Reject buttons.
  • Inputs. The same request, seed and approver in every scenario.

What differs

  • What decides the work. The form’s template and workflow, or policy, state and desired outcome.
  • Where required information lives. The form’s template, or the policy defaults.
  • Who keeps it current. The support team updates forms and templates by hand when the policy changes, or the agent reads the policy directly.
  • How Alex is told. Notifications in fixed wording, or messages in the chat.

Every call in AI-native passes four gates

Model

Reads, checks and asks. Each tick is a fresh call seeded with the saved request, never a transcript.

Orchestrator

Validates and audits every call and sets status from results, never from model text. It hands a clean request to the workflow.

Tools

A few narrow, typed tools. Policy arrives as data, never as raw Confluence text.

Identity provider

Independently checks the approval before any grant. The same check runs for AI automation.

If any gate says no, nothing changes.The model receives a structured error and the attempt is audited as denied.

The model reads and asks. The workflow acts.

Observe

Model: what does Alex have, is anything open, is the system healthy?

get_user_access
find_open_requests
get_system_status

Decide

Model: what does the policy require, and what is still missing?

get_access_policy
get_approval_status

Act

Workflow: ask for approval, tell Alex and grant. The model doesn’t choose these.

request_approval
notify_user
grant_access

Verify

Workflow: test access before saying it’s ready.

get_user_access

The model’s part ends once nothing is missing. The workflow runs until the outcome is verified, it is waiting on a person, or a failure needs intervention. A new message or an approval decision starts the next tick.

What this doesn’t show

  • That AI-native is cheaper or faster in production.Nothing here is measured at production scale.
  • That an agent loop is reliable across many request types.One request type is tested.
  • That forms and templates are useless.Some fields serve audit or compliance needs that aren’t modeled.
  • That a replayed run represents every live run.The demo shows one recorded run per scenario.

AI automation: a process, automated. AI-native: designed around an outcome.
